# Barcelona

## Overview
Barcelona combines a medieval old town, Gaudí's modernista architecture, city beaches and a famously late food culture. The grid-planned Eixample district holds most of the Gaudí landmarks; the old town (Ciutat Vella) holds the Gothic Quarter, El Born and La Rambla. Catalan and Spanish are both official languages.

## Best time to visit and climate
- May–June and September to early October are warm (highs 22–27 °C) and ideal for both sightseeing and the beach.
- July and August are hot and humid (highs around 29–31 °C) and crowded; many locals leave the city in August.
- Autumn brings the heaviest rain, especially October, usually as short and intense storms.
- Winter is mild (highs around 14 °C) and quiet, which makes it a good season for museums.

## Neighbourhoods to stay in
- Eixample: central and elegant, close to the Gaudí sights and Passeig de Gràcia, with excellent metro links.
- Gothic Quarter (Barri Gòtic) and El Born: historic lanes, bars and boutiques. Lively and sometimes noisy.
- Gràcia: a village feel with small squares, local restaurants and fewer tourists.
- Barceloneta: next to the beach. Touristy but convenient in summer.
- Poble-sec: at the foot of Montjuïc, with the tapas street Carrer de Blai.

## Getting around
- From the airport (BCN): the Aerobús reaches Plaça de Catalunya in about 35 minutes (approx. €7.25 one way). Metro line L9 Sud serves both terminals but needs a dedicated airport ticket (approx. €5.90). The R2 Nord train runs from Terminal 2. A taxi to the centre costs approx. €35–40.
- The T-casual card (10 journeys on metro, bus, tram and local trains in zone 1) costs approx. €12.55 and is much cheaper per ride than single tickets. Hola Barcelona passes (48 to 120 hours) suit heavy users.
- The centre is compact and very walkable.

## Typical costs
Approximate prices in 2026:
- Coffee: €1.70–2.50.
- "Menú del día" (weekday set lunch with a drink): €15–20.
- Tapas dinner: €25–40 per person.
- Mid-range hotel: €140–220 per night.
- Tourist tax: several euros per person per night (city plus regional surcharge), charged by the hotel.

## Food and drink
Pa amb tomàquet (bread rubbed with tomato), tapas and pintxos, seafood paella or black rice (arròs negre) by the sea, crema catalana, and vermouth before a late Sunday lunch. Lunch is around 14:00 and dinner rarely starts before 21:00.

## Practical tips
- Book timed tickets online for the Sagrada Família, the monumental zone of Park Güell and Casa Batlló; popular slots sell out days ahead.
- Pickpocketing is common on La Rambla, on the metro and at the beaches; keep phones out of back pockets.
- Many museums close on Mondays, and markets such as La Boqueria close on Sundays.
- Tipping is optional; rounding up or leaving 5–10% in restaurants is appreciated.

## Day trips
- Montserrat: a mountain monastery about 1 hour away by train and rack railway.
- Sitges: a beach town about 40 minutes away by train.
- Girona: a medieval old town under 40 minutes away by high-speed train.
