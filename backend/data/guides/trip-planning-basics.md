# Trip planning basics

## Daily budget rules of thumb
A rough daily spend per person for a Western European capital in 2026, excluding accommodation and flights:
- Budget: €50–70 — public transport, one sit-down meal, mostly free sights.
- Mid-range: €90–140 — two restaurant meals, two paid attractions and café stops.
- Comfort: €180 or more — fine dining, taxis and guided tours.
Accommodation is usually the largest cost: plan €110–260 per night for a mid-range double room, depending on the city and the season. Add a 10% buffer for unexpected costs.

## Building a daily itinerary
- Plan two or three major sights per day, plus time to wander. Big museums such as the Louvre or the Vatican Museums take half a day on their own.
- Group sights by neighbourhood to minimise travel time.
- Put timed-entry sights at the start of the day, when queues are shortest.
- Check closing days: many museums close one day a week, often Monday (Tuesday for the Louvre).
- Keep at least one indoor option for each day in case of rain.

## Planning around the weather
- Daily forecasts are reliable for about a week and indicative up to about two weeks ahead. Beyond that, plan from the typical climate for the season and stay flexible.
- On rainy days, prioritise museums, food halls, churches and covered markets; move viewpoints, parks and walking tours to drier days.
- Showers in Mediterranean cities are often short, and a rainy morning can clear by the afternoon.

## Money and payments
- Cards are accepted almost everywhere in these cities, and Stockholm is practically cashless.
- When a card terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency (dynamic currency conversion), decline and pay in the local currency; the exchange rate offered is usually worse.
- Attraction prices are normally per adult. Children and students often pay less, and EU residents under 26 get free or reduced entry at many state museums.

## Tipping at a glance
- Portugal, Spain and Italy: not expected; round up or leave 5–10% for good service.
- France: service is included; leave small change or a few euros for good service.
- Sweden: optional; rounding up or 5–10% is common in restaurants.

## Staying connected
Mobile roaming within the EU/EEA is included in most EU phone plans ("roam like at home"). Travellers from outside the EU should check their plan or buy a local eSIM.
