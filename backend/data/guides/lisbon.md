# Lisbon

## Overview
Lisbon spreads over seven hills on the north bank of the Tagus estuary. It is compact and walkable (but steep), and one of the more affordable Western European capitals. Most visitors split their time between the historic centre (Baixa, Chiado, Alfama), the riverside monuments of Belém, and evening neighbourhoods such as Bairro Alto and Cais do Sodré.

## Best time to visit and climate
- Spring (April–June) and early autumn (September–October) are the sweet spot: highs of 20–26 °C and fewer crowds than midsummer.
- July and August are hot and dry (highs often 28–32 °C) and busy. In June the Santo António festivities fill Alfama with music and grilled sardines.
- November to February is mild (highs around 15 °C) but it is the rainy season, so plan an indoor option for every day.
- The Atlantic breeze makes evenings cool even in summer; bring a light layer.

## Neighbourhoods to stay in
- Baixa / Chiado: central, flat by Lisbon standards and close to the metro. The easiest base for a first visit.
- Alfama: the oldest quarter, with steep lanes, fado houses and the castle. Atmospheric, but hilly and noisy on summer nights.
- Príncipe Real: quieter, leafy and upmarket, with boutique hotels.
- Cais do Sodré: nightlife and the Time Out Market, with direct trains to Belém and Cascais.
- Avenida da Liberdade: larger hotels along a grand boulevard with good transport links.

## Getting around
- From the airport (LIS): the red metro line runs from Aeroporto to the centre in about 20–25 minutes with one change. A single ride costs about €1.85, plus €0.50 for the rechargeable Navegante card. A taxi or ride-hail to Baixa costs roughly €15–20.
- Public transport covers metro, trams, buses and funiculars. A 24-hour pass for Carris buses/trams and the metro costs about €7.
- Tram 28 is the classic ride through Graça and Alfama, but it is crowded and a known pickpocket hotspot. Ride early in the morning.
- The Lisboa Card (24, 48 or 72 hours, from about €27) includes public transport, the trains to Sintra and Cascais, and free or discounted entry to many sights.
- Walking is the best way to see the centre, but the cobbled calçada pavements are slippery when wet: wear shoes with grip.

## Typical costs
Approximate prices in 2026:
- Espresso (bica): €1–1.50. Pastel de nata: €1.30–1.80.
- Lunch "prato do dia" (dish of the day): €10–14.
- Dinner at a mid-range restaurant: €25–35 per person with wine.
- Mid-range hotel: €110–170 per night for a double room.
- City tourist tax: about €4 per person per night (maximum 7 nights).

## Food and drink
Try pastéis de nata (the bakery in Belém is famous), bacalhau (salt cod) in its many forms, grilled sardines in summer, a bifana (pork sandwich) and a shot of ginjinha (sour-cherry liqueur) near Rossio. Restaurants often put bread, olives and cheese on the table (the couvert); it is charged only if you eat it.

## Practical tips
- Many museums and monuments close on Mondays, including Jerónimos Monastery and Belém Tower, so avoid planning Belém on a Monday.
- Tipping is not obligatory; rounding up or leaving 5–10% for good service is common.
- Sunsets from the miradouros (viewpoints) are free and among the best things to do in the city.

## Day trips
- Sintra: palaces and gardens in the hills, 40 minutes by train from Rossio. Go early and book Pena Palace tickets in advance.
- Cascais: seaside town 40 minutes by train from Cais do Sodré.
