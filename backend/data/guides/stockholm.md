# Stockholm

## Overview
Stockholm is built on 14 islands where Lake Mälaren meets the Baltic Sea. The medieval old town (Gamla Stan), the museum island of Djurgården and the creative Södermalm district are the main areas for visitors, and the archipelago of some 30,000 islands starts right at the city's edge. Sweden is close to cashless: cards and mobile payments are accepted almost everywhere, and some places don't take cash at all.

## Best time to visit and climate
- Late May to August brings long, light days (almost 18 hours of daylight in June) and highs of 20–25 °C. Midsummer, in late June, is a major holiday when the city empties.
- September is crisp and often sunny, with autumn colours.
- November to February is cold (highs around 0–3 °C) and dark (about 6 hours of daylight in December), but the Christmas markets in Gamla Stan and at Skansen make December atmospheric.
- Rain is moderate all year; July and August are the wettest months, mostly as showers.

## Neighbourhoods to stay in
- Norrmalm / City: the central station, shopping and the main transport hub.
- Gamla Stan: the cobbled old town. Charming but touristy, with limited hotel choice.
- Södermalm: creative and local, with SoFo's cafés and vintage shops, and great views from Monteliusvägen.
- Östermalm: elegant streets, the Östermalms Saluhall food hall and upscale dining.
- Djurgården: a green island with the main museums and only a few hotels.

## Getting around
- From Arlanda airport (ARN): the Arlanda Express train takes 18 minutes to Central Station (approx. SEK 330 one way for adults, with discounts for under-26s and groups). The Flygbussarna airport coach takes about 45 minutes (approx. SEK 130). The SL commuter train (pendeltåg) is the cheapest option: an SL ticket plus an airport access fee, approx. SEK 160 in total.
- SL runs the metro (tunnelbana), buses, trams, commuter trains and some ferries, including the Djurgården ferry from Slussen. A single ticket valid for 75 minutes costs about SEK 43; a 24-hour pass about SEK 175 and a 72-hour pass about SEK 350. You can tap a contactless bank card at the gates.
- The metro stations double as an art gallery; T-Centralen, Kungsträdgården and Stadion are highlights.
- Archipelago boats (Waxholmsbolaget, Strömma) are not included in SL passes.

## Typical costs
Approximate prices in 2026:
- Coffee: SEK 40–55. A fika (coffee and a cinnamon bun): SEK 70–90.
- Weekday "dagens lunch" (lunch special including salad, bread and coffee): SEK 125–160.
- Dinner at a mid-range restaurant: SEK 350–500 per person with a drink.
- Mid-range hotel: SEK 1,400–2,200 per night.

## Food and drink
Meatballs with lingonberries, toast skagen, pickled herring, gravlax, cinnamon and cardamom buns, and seasonal treats like the semla (February) and crayfish parties (August). Alcohol stronger than 3.5% is sold for takeaway only at Systembolaget shops, which close early on Saturdays and are closed on Sundays.

## Practical tips
- Weekday lunch deals are the best-value meal of the day.
- Many museums close on Mondays outside the summer season.
- Tipping is optional; rounding up or about 5–10% in restaurants is common.
- Tap water is excellent.

## Day trips
- Vaxholm: an archipelago town about 1 hour away by boat from Strömkajen.
- Uppsala: a university city with a cathedral, about 40 minutes away by train.
- Drottningholm Palace: the royal family's residence, reached by boat across Lake Mälaren in summer, or by metro and bus.
