# Rome

## Overview
Rome layers 2,500 years of history: ancient ruins around the Colosseum and the Forum, Renaissance and Baroque piazzas in the historic centre, and the Vatican, a separate state with St. Peter's Basilica and the Vatican Museums. The centre is walkable; plan each day around one area to avoid crossing the city repeatedly.

## Best time to visit and climate
- April–May and late September–October are warm (highs 20–26 °C) and offer the best balance of weather and crowds.
- June to August is hot (highs 30–33 °C) and crowded: sightsee early and late, and rest at midday.
- October and November are the wettest months, with heavy showers.
- Winter is mild (highs 12–14 °C) and the quietest season for museums.

## Neighbourhoods to stay in
- Centro Storico: around the Pantheon and Piazza Navona; almost everything is walkable.
- Monti: hip and central, close to the Colosseum and the metro.
- Trastevere: cobbled lanes and trattorias across the river. Lively at night, but with no metro.
- Prati: calm, near the Vatican, with good restaurants and metro line A.
- Testaccio: local food culture and a market hall; less touristy.

## Getting around
- From Fiumicino airport (FCO): the Leonardo Express train reaches Roma Termini in 32 minutes for €14. Regional FL1 trains to Trastevere and Ostiense are cheaper (about €8). Taxis charge a fixed fare of about €55 to anywhere inside the Aurelian walls.
- Public transport has three metro lines plus buses and trams. A single ticket costs €1.50 and is valid for 100 minutes (one metro ride); a 24-hour pass costs about €9. Contactless bank cards work at the metro gates.
- The historic centre has few metro stops, so most people walk.

## Typical costs
Approximate prices in 2026:
- Espresso standing at the bar: €1.20–1.50 (table service costs more).
- Pizza al taglio or another takeaway lunch: €6–10.
- Trattoria dinner: €25–40 per person.
- Mid-range hotel: €130–200 per night.
- City tax: about €6–7.50 per person per night in mid-range hotels (maximum 10 nights).

## Food and drink
The four classic Roman pastas are carbonara, cacio e pepe, amatriciana and gricia. Also try supplì (fried rice balls), carciofi alla giudia (fried artichokes) in season, and gelato. Many restaurants add a small "coperto" (cover charge) per person.

## Practical tips
- Book timed entry for the Colosseum and the Vatican Museums well ahead. The Borghese Gallery only admits visitors with a reservation.
- The Vatican Museums are closed on Sundays, except the last Sunday of the month. St. Peter's Basilica is free but has long security queues.
- Churches require covered shoulders and knees.
- Refill water bottles at the "nasoni" public fountains; the water is drinkable.
- Tipping is not expected; rounding up is fine.

## Day trips
- Ostia Antica: Rome's ancient port, about 35 minutes away by train.
- Tivoli: the fountains of Villa d'Este and Hadrian's Villa, about 1 hour away by bus or train.
- Orvieto: a hill town about 1 hour 15 minutes away by train.
